THW ILLUSION ENGINE

I no longer see the world as it is, I see it as I can tolerate it. Everyday without noticing it, 
my mind edits reality in my favour. It softens what threathens me, reshapes what exposes me,
and quietly replaces what is uncomfortable with what is easier to accept. This is not because 
I am weak, but because I am built to survive myself. Beneath my thoughts, beneath my reasoning, 
there is a system at work, one that does not ask whether ideas are true, only whether they are
bearable. And if I have never questioned this, then most of what I call clarity may simply be 
comfort in disguise.
My ife is not defined only by the situations i find myself in, but by how i meet myself in 
those situations. At its core. living is inseperable from discomfort. Pain, in its many forms, 
is not an interruption of life; it is one of its primary drivers. It signals change, 
demands adaption, and forces movement. Some people learn to work with it, to interpret it, 
even to transform it. Others become rather entangled, reacting instead of responding. The 
difference is often not in the intensity of the experience, but in the perception of it. 
A good example is a child who can invest hours running, jumping, falling and rising again, engaging 
in what is essentially continous physical exertion but without  labeling it as suffering. To the 
child, it is play. An adult faced with the same physical demands now has so many names for it. They
can call it effort, strain or even punishment. Yeah I know most adults have other mental
processes too that harvests attention as much, but the activity have not changed, only the 
interpretation has. And that alone quietly determines behaviour. 
Beneath these interpretations lies a deeper mechanism, it is the one that operates automatically,
invisibly, and with remarkable consistensy. Each of us carries this private biological engine within
us, whose primary function is not to reveal reality but to protect the ego, It does not prioritise 
truth, logic, or accuracy. it prioritises emotional survival. Whenever reality threatens our
sense of control, identity, or self-worth, this system intervenes. It generates subtle distortions 
micro storries, justifications, softened interpretations, anything that can reduce psychological 
exposure. Sometimes, it is not enough to feel like deception, but is definitely enough to
avoid discomforts and hinder actions. I call it the illusion engine. Its influence is constant, but 
rarely obvious. It does not announce itself as distortion; it presents itself as 
reasonable thoughts, and because of it most of its work goes unquestioned.
Over time, these adjustments accumulate, shaping our perceptions of ourselves, others, even the world.
What we call "perspective" is often just the output of this engine at work. 
Now here is the real thing, the distinction is not between those who experience the illution engine and 
those who do not. We all do. The differnce lies in awareness. The ability, to notice when this automatic 
process is shaping our perception, especially in moments of of decision, is what begins to seperate our 
experience of clarity from distortion. In many ways, this is where the divergance between childlike 
directness and adult complexity begins. not usually in intelligence, but in interference. "Smoke" qs 
referenced in this book as a metaphor for these distortions, the subtle obscuring layer that forms 
when we continously defer to the illusion engine. Smoke does not defer reality, it conceals it. It blurs 
edges, softens consequences, and limits visibility. The more we rely on it, the less we clearly see the 
world and ourselves in it. And the cost of this blindness is not neutral. Ignorance of the illusion engine 
does not make us immune to its effects. On the contrary, it makes us more vulnerable to not only our own 
distortions but also to those who understand how to navigate, influence, or exploit them.

After extensive observation and reflection, the patterns of this mechanism began to come to me. The 
illusion engine does not operate randomly, it expresses itself through recurring modes. In this work
let us examine four of its most common forms: comfort fabrication, narrative preference, social smoke, 
and invisible scripts. 

COMFORT FABRICATION

Imagine a place with no system except rhythmic chaos and utter randomness, even 
though for some unclear reasons no one accepts it yet. You know very well that nothing works in a 
systemic sense even though that the modern time thrives on systems, you follow through with the promise 
of a system that was made before you were born, by people who did not have you in mind. A promise that was
never going to be fulfilled. You have experienced grandparents and parents as they regret beleiving in that 
promise, even your own parents too. You know how it is, they realised they only had one lives and made a 
choice - to "enjoy it while it lasts," instead of fight for it. so even in their lamentations, except for 
occasional loud public contributions, they did not truly provided any solutions. Now you are growing in 
that same environments and faced with almost the same options they had. Will you make different - difficult - 
choices or join the comfort narrative? unfortunately for most people, that choice is easy and almost already 
made. Well that is the situation i have lived with too. There is reality, and there is what we tell ourselves. 
That gap between them is where comfort fabrication comes in. The illusion engine wants only psychological 
survival, therefore it protects the ego the same way the body protects a wound: by covering it, softening 
contact and avoiding pressure. one of its most subtle but powerful tools, Comfort fabrication, is the 
unconcious rewriting of reality into versions we can emmotionally tolerate.
when i approach the edge of discomfort, whether failure, fear, inadequacy or loss of control, my mind 
intervenes. not with lies obvious enough to reject, but with explanations gentle enough to accept. 
"i did not really fail - my timing was just not right." "it is not fear, i am just not ready yet." "it is 
not avoidance, this place is simply not meant for me." These narratives feel reasonable, even wise, because 
they are designed to to preserve my sense of self while quietly removing the urgency to change. 
in this way, comfort is just a substitute for clarity. i am not really deceiving myself, i am only being 
protected from a version of reality that would demand action, responsibility, or transformation. and so 
i remain intact, but unchanged. the illusion is not dramatic, it is functional. it allows me to continue, 
to cope to maintain identity - but at a cost. Because every softened truth delays a necessary confrontation, 
and every comforting story postpopnes the moment where i must decide to either remain who i am or become who 
reality/the world is asking me to.

NARRATIVE PREFERENCE 

Humans do not naturally seek truth, we seek coherence. and stories provide that far more efficiently than 
facts ever could. Facts are cold, fragmented, often indifferent to our personal significance. They do not 
expain why things happen, nor do they assign meaning, justice, or direction. Stories do. They come preloaded 
with structures, heroes and villains, causes and consequences, beginings and ends. Most importantly, they 
leave rooms for us to exixt in a way that feels defined and protected. Truth on the other hand, is frequently 
chaotic, unresolved and - perharps the most threatening of it all - ego free. It does not care who we are in 
the narrative. it simply is. so the world leans towards interpretation over observation and it is not because 
we are irrational but because stories offer psychological stability. they organise randomness into something 
we can emotionally survive. But while doing it, they also quietly bends reality in our favour. we do not 
usually tell stories about the world, we tell stories of ourselves within it. And those stories tend to 
protect our image, justify our position, and reduce our exposure. we are less interested in what is true 
than in what is livable. 
Now the crazy part. this tendency scales far beyond the individual. Entire societies are built on shared 
narratives that feels self-evident but are, at the core, condtructed agreements. history have become a story 
of origins and ownership and boarders, immaginary lines that seperates "us" from "them," as though the 
earth itself recorgniszes the distinction. Money have become a symbol that claims to measure value in real time, 
as if worth can be universally quantified. Time is flattened into a straight line with the past behind us and 
the future ahead, despite the cyclical patterns embedded in nature and human behaviour. Even success is 
narrativized into something visible, comparable, and externally validated, rather than internally experienced. 
none of these are inherently false in function, since they work. But they are still stories. And like all 
stories, they simplify what is fundamentally complex. They create an illusion of order in a world that does 
not guarantee it.
As children, we encounter reality with a kind of raw clarity that allows experience before interpretations. 
But as we grow that clarity is gradually replaced by inhereted narratives absorbed from cultures, institutions,
and repitition. We are thought, subtly and persistently, that louder signals matter more than quiet ones, that 
external validation outweighs inteernal alignment, that identity is tied to location, status, and comparison. 
we begin to see ourselves and others no longer as fluid human beings, but as fixed roles within a larger 
script. to the child reading this, all the friends you see as you look around, and even the ones 
you imagine making plenty of that makes you excited about the future, eventually becomes your competitive 
"other" despite sharing the same ground initially. progress have become a race, stillness a failure and 
meaning, something to be earned, displayed, and recorgnised. Over time, these narratives converge into what 
feels like an objective moral structure, a collective agreement about what is right, wrong, valuable, and not. 
But beeneath that agreement lies a quieter, more uncomfortable reality: in a world without inherent narrative, 
everyone is operating from a story that makes sense to them. Each perspective is internally consistent, shaped 
by its own assumptions, experiences, and needs. This means that conflict is not always a clash between truth 
and falsehood, but often a collision between different stories, each one feeling justified from the inside. 
this is the hidden cost of narrative preference. it gives us orientation, but at the expense of direct 
perception. It allows societies to function, but also makes genuine understanding more difficult.
Because once a story feels like reality, questioning it no longer feels like curiousity but threat. And so we 
defend, reinforce, and pass it on. Not because it is true. Because it holds us together.

SOCIAL SMOKE

Beyond the distortions we generate internally, there is another layer that is more difuse, more prevasive 
and more difficult to detect. It does not originte within the individual mind, but from the collective 
environment we inhabit. It  is the haze produced by culture itself. This is what i refer to as the social 
smoke. Unlike the personal smoke which are inhaled to protect the individual ego, social smoke is shared. 
It moves through groups, systems, and institutions, shaping perception at scale. It tells us what matters, 
what is desirable, and what is acceptable, often before we have the chance to question it. This is arguably the 
most powerful, and because it is collectively reinforced, it almost never appears as distortions. It appears 
as normal. In this environment, identity is increasingly externalised. Trends substitute self-definition and
belonging outweighs accuracy. Validation which is measured through attention, agreement, or visibility, 
gradually takes the role of evidence. What is repeated often enough begins to feel true, not because it has 
been examined, but because it has been collectively accepted. Under these conditions, individuals now model 
themselves after abstractions - crowds they do not know, standards they did not create, expectations they 
did not conciously coose. This is often mistaken for self expression, when in reality it is allignment. 
The distinction is subtle but significant - one emerges from internal clarity, the other from external pressure.
this dynamic does not exist in isolation, it is shaped by the historical and structural context that we inherit.
To be born in the early 21st century is to enter a world already layered with the unresolved outcomes of 
previous eras. the systems, institutions, and cultural assumptions that organise modern life were not created 
in a vacum. They are the results of earlier attempts to establish order, meaning and control. Over time, 
however, these systems evolve. Their original intentions become secondary to their continued operation.
Efficiency replaces reflection. Growth replaces purpose. In the end what remains is a world that functions, but 
not always in alignment with the individuals within it. this is where distortion deepens. Now we do not merely
live within these inherited beliefs, we often identify with them. we defend them, participate in them, and
derive meaning from them, even when they no longer serve the purpose for which they were originally formed.
The familiarity of the system becomes its own justification. Questioning it begins to feel unnecessary, even
disruptive. But familiarity is not the same as truth. The social smoke operates by maintaining this quiet 
continuity. It allows outdated assumptions to persist under modern Conditions. It preserves patterns long after 
their context has changed. And because it is everywhere - embedded in language, media, norms, and expectations - 
it is rarely experienced as influence. It is experienced as reality. 
Now this does not mean that all systems are inherently flawed or must be rejected outright. Many of them 
continue to provide structure, coordination, and stability. But it does suggest that unexamined trust carries 
its own risk. Systems that were once built in search of meaning can, over time, become optimised for entirely 
different outcomes. and when that shift goes unnoticed, the individual is left navigating a framework that may 
no longer reflect their needs, values or reality. To see through social smoke is not to detatch from society,
but to recorgnise the difference between perception and absorption. It is to understand that what is widely 
accepted is not always deeply true, and that clarity in such environments requires more than agreement. It 
requires awareness. 

INVISIBLE SCRIPTS

A child does not choose the conditions that shape them. They do not choose the economic reality they are born 
into the cultural expectations that placed upon them, or the historical weight carried by the environment. 
They do not choose the fears they inherit, the silences they learn to maintain, or the ambitions they are 
taught to pursue. Yet over time, these elements become deeply embedded - so much so that questioning them 
feels like questioning oneself. this is about inherited beliefs - the language we use, Our values, fears, 
expectations, definitions of success, ideas of right and wrong. These do not arrive as options or discovery, 
it is the result of inheritance. They arrive as givens. And quietly, consistently, and without announcement, 
they form the basis of how we see the world. By the time we become ourselves as individuals, much of that 
foundation is already in place. these are invisible scripts. The unspoken codea passed through family, 
cultural environment, and early experience. They do not present themselves as instructions, yet they guide 
behaviour with remarkable precision. They shape what feels natural, what feels possible, what feels acceptable. 
They define the boundaries of imagination without ever appearing as limits. and because they are internalised 
so early, they are rarely experienced as external influence. They feel like identity. 
In many parts of the world, especially in societies that have experienced layered histories of control,
disruption and imposed order, these scripts carry additional weight. they are not only cultural, they are 
structural. They are reinforced by systems, institutions. and narratives that have outlived the conditions 
that created them. What began as adaptation - or sometimes even survival - gradually hardens into norm.
And the things that are repeated long enough begin to feel inevitable. This is how I came to live 
with frameworks I did not design yet felt compelled to defend. It was not because I conciously chose them, but 
because those frameworks had shaped my sense of stability, belonging and identity. To question them is not 
merely ro reconsider an idea - it is to risk disorientation. It is to step outside what is familiar without 
immediate replacement. And for many, the cost feels too high. so the scripts continue to run. Influencing
decicions, relationships, ambitions, and limitations. They determine what is pursued, and what is avoided, 
odren without awareness. We all start off beleiving we were acing freely, but over time, unknowingly, we 
unknowingly move along paths that were laid long before we had the ability to choose. This is not a failure 
of intelligence, it is the natural consequence of early conditioning.
But recorgnition changes the equation. To see these scripts is to create distance from them. Not to reject 
everything that has been inherited, but to examine it. To ask what still holds? what no longer serves?
and what was never truely mine to bigin with? Because the moment something becomes visible, it is no longer 
absolute. And in the space - between what was given and what is chosen - something else is possible. 
Not a complete escape from influence, but a more concious participation in it.

THE FEAR BEHIND THE ILLUSION

Beneath every distortion, every softened truth, every carefully constructed interpretation. There is something 
quieter holding it in place. Fear. It is not the loud, visible kind - the subtle instinctively resistance that 
operatees just below awareness. It does not announce itself directly. Instead, it speaks in implications: "If 
i see things clearly, i will have to response, "If i respond, i will have to change," "if i change, 
i will loose the explanations that once protected me," and without those explanations, i will have to face 
myself without mediation. This is the quiet contract at the core of the illusion engine. Designed to preserve,
to maintain a version of myself that feels stable, continous and defensible. Illusion in this way, is a form
of protection. Not error. It is a form of protection that shields the psyche from the full weight of responsibility.
The kind that comes not from external demands, but from seeing clearly and knowing that something must be done. 
And this is how my mind adjusts. Not completely detached from reality, but enough to soften its demands. enough 
to delay actions and remain in. So now, the pattern is be visible. The comforts we fabricate, the stories we 
prefer, the social high we navigate, the scripts we inherit without question. They all point back to one center,
Avoidance of exposure. Not weekness or ignorance, but protection from consequence. There is no need for shame in 
this recorgnition. These mechanisms are not personal failures, they are human defaults. To see them is not to 
condemn yourself but to understand the conditions under which you have been operating. More importantly, to realise 
that they are not absolute. Because once something is seen clearly, it looses its ability to operate unnoticed. 
The illusion engine depends on subtelty. it depends on remaining unexamined. the moment the patterns become visible, 
they do not disappear, but can no longer move with the same authority. You begin to notice the shift. The pause before 
the excuse forms, the space between the discomfort and interpretation. The moment where a tiny story could be told,
but is not immediately beleived. That space is where change begins. Deliberate, not forced or dramatic. Because 
clarity does not demand perfection, it only removes the hiding place. And once there is nowhere left to hide, 
there is only one direction left to move. Forward, clearly.
